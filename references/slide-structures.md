# Slide structures by purpose

Pick the closest structure, then adapt slide count to the time limit (roughly one slide per minute, minus an intro and Q&A).

## 1. Business pitch (default, 10 slides)

1. Hook — the one thing that makes this worth listening to
2. Problem — who feels it, how much it hurts
3. Solution — what you built, in one sentence
4. How it works — the mechanics, no fluff
5. Market — size, segments, momentum
6. Competition — landscape and your wedge
7. Traction — metrics, logos, growth
8. Business model — how money is made
9. Team — why this group can win
10. Ask — what you need and what it buys

## 2. Status report (8 slides)

1. Context — where we are in the plan
2. Wins since last report
3. Metrics dashboard
4. Issues and risks
5. What's behind schedule (and why)
6. Decisions needed from leadership
7. Next steps
8. Ask / open questions

## 3. Training / explainer (10 slides)

1. Why this matters to the audience
2. Core concept in one sentence
3. Common misconceptions
4. How it works — step by step
5. Live example or walkthrough
6. Do's and don'ts
7. Practice scenario
8. Recap of key points
9. Resources for going deeper
10. Q&A

## 4. Data-heavy report (12 slides)

1. Executive summary — the three numbers that matter
2. Methodology (one slide, honest and short)
3. Headline finding 1
4. Headline finding 2
5. Headline finding 3
6. What changed vs. last period
7. Breakdown by segment
8. Breakdown by region
9. What the data doesn't say (limitations)
10. Implications
11. Recommendations
12. Appendix pointers

## 5. Proposal / RFP response (12 slides)

1. Executive summary
2. Understanding of the requirement
3. Approach and methodology
4. Work plan and timeline
5. Team and roles
6. Relevant experience
7. Differentiators
8. Risks and mitigations
9. Pricing / commercial summary
10. Deliverables
11. Next steps
12. Contact and appendix

## 6. Product launch (10 slides)

1. The moment — one line that captures what's new and why now
2. Why this product exists — the problem or market shift that made it necessary
3. The reveal — what it is, in one sentence
4. Hero feature 1 — the demo moment
5. Hero feature 2 — the second demo moment
6. Hero feature 3 / supporting capabilities
7. Under the hood — engineering quality, reliability, security story
8. Availability — pricing, platforms, rollout timeline
9. Roadmap — what ships next and why it matters
10. Call to action — try it, buy it, follow it

## 7. Annual review (10 slides)

1. The year in one line — headline outcome
2. Goals and how we scored against them
3. The numbers that mattered most
4. Wins worth celebrating
5. Misses and what they cost
6. Root causes — honest, no blame
7. What we learned
8. Stop / start / continue
9. Priorities for next year
10. Ask / decisions needed from leadership

## 8. Course lecture (12 slides)

1. Learning objective — what learners can do by the end
2. Prerequisites — what we assume you already know
3. Agenda — three to five chunks with rough timing
4. Concept 1 — the idea in one sentence
5. Concept 1 — worked example
6. Concept 2 — the idea in one sentence
7. Concept 2 — worked example
8. Common mistakes and how to avoid them
9. Hands-on exercise — with a visible timebox
10. Check for understanding — three quick questions
11. Summary — key takeaways in one breath
12. Homework / next steps

## 9. Case study (8 slides)

1. Customer and context — who they are and what they sell
2. The challenge — the pain, in numbers
3. Alternatives considered — why they chose us
4. The solution — what we built together
5. Timeline — from kickoff to value, briefly
6. Results — before and after metrics
7. Proof in their words — quote or qualitative signal
8. Why this matters to you — relevance for the audience

## 10. Workshop (8 slides)

1. Goal and agenda — what we will produce together
2. Ground rules — timeboxes, decide and move on
3. Context-setting input — ten minutes of shared framing
4. Activity 1 — instructions and timebox
5. Share-back — patterns across the room
6. Activity 2 — instructions and timebox
7. Consolidation — what we decided
8. Next actions — owners and due dates

## 11. Investor update (7 slides)

1. Headline — the one number and the one decision the update exists for
2. Metrics — the dashboard, same metrics every period
3. What worked — two or three specific wins
4. What did not — stated plainly, with the response
5. Asks — where the board or investor can help
6. Cash and runway — the unemotional version
7. What happens next — the next milestone and its date

## 12. Quarterly business review (10 slides)

1. Quarter in one line — result against target
2. Scorecard — every committed metric, target vs. actual
3. Revenue and cost drivers
4. Customer story — one account, start to result
5. What over-performed and why
6. What under-performed and why
7. Risks entering next quarter
8. Decisions needed now
9. Next quarter commitments
10. Ask / open discussion

## 13. Client proposal (10 slides)

1. Their situation — restate the problem in their words
2. The cost of inaction — quantified
3. Our approach — three phases, one line each
4. Scope — what is included, what is not
5. Timeline — milestones and dependencies
6. Proof — closest comparable engagement
7. Team — who does what, named
8. Commercials — options, not a single price
9. Risks and how we handle them
10. Next step — the specific action and date

## 14. Technical design review (10 slides)

1. Problem statement and constraints
2. Requirements — functional and non-functional
3. Options considered — with the trade-off table
4. Chosen design — one diagram, top level
5. Data model and interfaces
6. Failure modes and mitigation
7. Security and privacy considerations
8. Migration and rollout plan
9. Open questions for reviewers
10. Decision requested

## 15. Post-mortem (8 slides)

1. What happened — timeline of facts only
2. Impact — users, money, duration
3. Detection — how it was found, how long it took
4. Root cause — the mechanism, not the person
5. What went well in the response
6. What went badly
7. Action items with owners and dates
8. What we will verify next time

## 16. All-hands update (8 slides)

1. Where we are — one line on the state of the business
2. The numbers everyone should know
3. What changed since last time
4. Customer moment — one story from the field
5. Team recognition — specific, short
6. What we are betting on next
7. How it affects you — what changes for each team
8. Questions

## Choosing a structure

| If the deck is about | Use |
|----------------------|-----|
| Raising money from a new investor | 1 Business pitch |
| Reporting progress upward | 2 Status report |
| Explaining a concept to learners | 3 Training / explainer |
| Defending an analysis | 4 Data-heavy report |
| Responding to a formal requirement | 5 Proposal / RFP response |
| Announcing something new | 6 Product launch |
| Reviewing twelve months | 7 Annual review |
| Teaching a class | 8 Course lecture |
| Showing what a customer achieved | 9 Case study |
| Running a working session | 10 Workshop |
| Updating existing investors | 11 Investor update |
| Reviewing one quarter | 12 Quarterly business review |
| Winning one client | 13 Client proposal |
| Getting a design approved | 14 Technical design review |
| Explaining a failure | 15 Post-mortem |
| Updating the whole company | 16 All-hands update |

## Rules of thumb

- One slide per major message. If a slide needs more than five bullets, split it.
- Slide 1 must state the outcome, not just the topic.
- The "ask" slide should come last, never first.
- For every data claim, keep the source one glance away (footnote or appendix).
