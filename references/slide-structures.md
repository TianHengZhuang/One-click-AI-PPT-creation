# Slide structures by purpose

Pick the closest structure, then adapt slide count to the time limit (roughly one slide per minute, minus an intro and Q&A).

## 1. Business pitch (default, 10 slides)

1. Hook — the one thing that makes this worth listening to
2. Problem — who feels it, how much it hurts
3. Solution — what you built, in one sentence
4. How it works — the mechanics, no fluff
5. Market — size, segments, momentum
6. Competition — landscape and your wedge
7. Traction — metrics, logos, growth
8. Business model — how money is made
9. Team — why this group can win
10. Ask — what you need and what it buys

## 2. Status report (8 slides)

1. Context — where we are in the plan
2. Wins since last report
3. Metrics dashboard
4. Issues and risks
5. What's behind schedule (and why)
6. Decisions needed from leadership
7. Next steps
8. Ask / open questions

## 3. Training / explainer (10 slides)

1. Why this matters to the audience
2. Core concept in one sentence
3. Common misconceptions
4. How it works — step by step
5. Live example or walkthrough
6. Do's and don'ts
7. Practice scenario
8. Recap of key points
9. Resources for going deeper
10. Q&A

## 4. Data-heavy report (12 slides)

1. Executive summary — the three numbers that matter
2. Methodology (one slide, honest and short)
3. Headline finding 1
4. Headline finding 2
5. Headline finding 3
6. What changed vs. last period
7. Breakdown by segment
8. Breakdown by region
9. What the data doesn't say (limitations)
10. Implications
11. Recommendations
12. Appendix pointers

## 5. Proposal / RFP response (12 slides)

1. Executive summary
2. Understanding of the requirement
3. Approach and methodology
4. Work plan and timeline
5. Team and roles
6. Relevant experience
7. Differentiators
8. Risks and mitigations
9. Pricing / commercial summary
10. Deliverables
11. Next steps
12. Contact and appendix

## Rules of thumb

- One slide per major message. If a slide needs more than five bullets, split it.
- Slide 1 must state the outcome, not just the topic.
- The "ask" slide should come last, never first.
- For every data claim, keep the source one glance away (footnote or appendix).
