# Copywriting guide

Rules and before/after examples for presentation copy.

## The rules

1. **Active voice.** "We reduced onboarding time by 40%", not "Onboarding time was reduced by 40%".
2. **Numbers first.** Lead with the metric, then the context: "18% revenue growth from enterprise renewals", not "Enterprise renewals helped us grow revenue".
3. **One idea per bullet.** If a bullet has an "and", it probably has two ideas.
4. **Short sentences.** Under ~15 words per sentence. One sentence per bullet where possible.
5. **No audience-hostile jargon.** If the audience wouldn't say the word in a meeting, don't put it on the slide.
6. **Specific beats generic.** "Cut support tickets by 3,200/month" beats "improved customer support".
7. **Headlines are claims.** A headline should be something the audience could argue with — that means it carries information. "Revenue" is not a headline. "Q3 revenue grew 18% on enterprise renewals" is.

## Before / after

| Before (weak) | After (strong) |
|---|---|
| Revenue increased | Q3 revenue grew 18% on enterprise renewals |
| We help customers save money | Customers cut procurement costs by $2.1M/yr on average |
| Our product is easy to use | New users run their first report in under 90 seconds |
| Strong market opportunity | The segment is growing 23% YoY and is still under-penetrated |
| We have a great team | Team shipped 3 enterprise pilots in 6 months, all renewed |

## Speaker notes

Each slide gets 2–3 spoken sentences:

1. Say the headline claim out loud (as a sentence, not a title).
2. Add the supporting number or story behind it.
3. Bridge to the next slide ("which brings me to…").

Notes are for the presenter, so write them like a person talks, not like a document reads.
